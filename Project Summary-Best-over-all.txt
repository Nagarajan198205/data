Project Summary (<=100 words) 

  

Since 2022, Cognizant has replaced hand-run regression across eight public sector delivery streams with automated pipelines. Across Portal, Dynamics and E2E, 97.66 per cent of scripts are automated. IPAFFS and Risk Engine regression fell from four days to half a day. Five service teams now own their regression on a shared Salesforce platform. Test effectiveness is 99.94 per cent across the lifecycle. Thirty-three transformation initiatives are live or in progress. A team of 62, 39 per cent women, delivered this across services processing 1.8 million Export Health Certificates and enabling £657m of plant health exports annually. 

  

  

1. The Project and Why It Mattered 

  

We are Cognizant, and since July 2022 we have tested public sector digital services for a UK government client. The export service has processed more than 1.8 million Export Health Certificates across 1,552 digital export journeys, with 93 per cent customer satisfaction. The plant health export service has enabled £657m of exports. NIRMS automation has delivered 19.5 FTE of efficiency savings. IPAFFS handles import controls for products, animals, food and feed. Air quality services reach citizens and businesses across the UK. 

  

A defect that escapes testing is a delayed shipment, a failed border check or incorrect public health information. Testing was not keeping pace: IPAFFS regression took four days per cycle, PDF checks were manual, and one team's code push on the shared Salesforce platform could break another team's features. Our 2026 transformation plan set a measurable target for each problem. 

  

  

2. Programme Goals, Importance and What We Achieved 

  

The programme covered eight delivery streams: Trade (Portal, Dynamics, E2E), Common Platform, Operations Proving (IPAFFS, Risk Engine, PLP), Air Quality, PHES, NIRMS and the Salesforce Single Customer View platform. 

  

- Portal, Dynamics and E2E regression: 97.66 per cent automated, all critical E2E scenarios covered, up from 88.86 per cent in 2022 (Exhibit 1). 

- IPAFFS and Risk Engine: all four CHED types automated; regression fell from four days to half a day (Exhibit 4). 

- AI-driven coverage expansion at roughly three times the previous pace; impact-based selection cutting runs by 40 to 60 per cent; script maintenance effort down 60 per cent (Exhibit 8). 

- Migration test case generation: 60 to 75 per cent faster, saving £6,930 per cycle (Exhibit 8). 

- AI test case generation: from 166 person-days to 36, a 78.3 per cent cut (Exhibit 7). 

- Posit upgrade automation: in progress at 40:60 today against a 90:10 target (Exhibit 7). 

  

Test effectiveness across the lifecycle is 99.94 per cent: of 3,196 valid defects found since 2022, only two reached production across 18 releases in 2026. The customer satisfaction overall score was 5 out of 5 and the Net Promoter Score was 10 out of 10. 

  

  

3. Vision, Public-Sector Impact and Forward Thinking 

  

The services we test carry statutory obligations: exporters need certificates to move goods, importers need IPAFFS clearance, and citizens rely on air quality data for health decisions. The export service has processed more than 1.8 million Export Health Certificates, supporting UK agri-food exports worth £23.6bn annually to the EU. Digital adoption reached 99.99 per cent, transitioning exporters from paper to digital certification. Certificate issuance times fell by up to 44 per cent, with turnaround shortened by one to three days. NIRMS automation delivered 19.5 FTE of efficiency savings with 66 per cent of cases now automated. 

  

Our response was to move quality ownership as close to the code as possible. On the Single Customer View platform, each of the five service teams tags its own business-critical Playwright tests @platform-regression, and the platform pack discovers and runs them automatically with no hand-off. A Node.js scaffolding utility sets up a new team in minutes from one template. 

  

Shift-left testing at Air Quality removed dependence on third-party systems, letting us run more than 100 business scenarios on demand. The customer formally recognised this as a process innovation in 2026 (Exhibit 3). When the production site was hit by a DDoS attack, our team restored the service quickly and the client extended our remit. PLP migration QA signed off on schedule with no critical issues at go-live. 

  

Thirty-three transformation initiatives across Automation and DevOps, Gen AI and Reporting and QA are live or in progress, each starting from a testing problem.

5. Approach, Methodology and Technology Choices 

  

Agile was chosen for PHES and Trade streams to match iterative feature delivery. Operations Proving used waterfall because CHED regulatory workflows required fixed-scope validation before each release. Test scope covers in-sprint and integration testing through to API, accessibility, cross-browser, cross-device, performance, security and business acceptance testing, with BrowserStack covering Android and iOS. We report 100 per cent traceability of requirements across all 18-plus active services. 

  

Tools were chosen by layer. E2E uses Selenium and EasyRepro, with Reqnroll and NUnit replacing SpecFlow; the Dynamics team moved to Playwright. Air Quality uses WebdriverIO with Allure. APIs use RestSharp and Postman. Pipelines run in Azure DevOps. All tools are open-source. 

  

AI was applied in three places: GitHub Copilot cut Air Quality scripting effort by 50 per cent across 120-plus cases; our VS Code AI test case generator turns acceptance criteria into import-ready Azure DevOps test cases; and across wider work, AI reduced manual test case generation from 166 person-days to 36. 

  

  

6. Delivery with Stakeholders 

  

All eight delivery streams completed testing within agreed sprint timelines in 2026. No deliverable slippage was recorded across 18 production releases. The release tracker, built on Microsoft Lists and Power Automate, replaced a shared Excel sheet that caused version conflicts and required manual chasing. It gives real-time visibility of slot availability, sends automatic notifications and keeps a full audit history. It was demonstrated to five service teams and Cognizant management; all gave positive feedback and the client has shown interest in adopting it more widely. 

  

For the Risk Engine and Cloning archival work, the client praised seven months of sustained effort and said proper testing made it possible, expressing interest in seeing the delivery pattern adopted more broadly (Exhibit 8). The PLP migration was delivered on time with all critical functionality validated and no critical issues at go-live. Budget: open-source tools throughout; no separate tooling spend. 

  

  

7. Overcoming Challenges 

  

The Single Customer View platform is shared by five service teams. Any code push could break another team's features. Service teams now tag business-critical Playwright cases @platform-regression; the platform pack finds and runs them with no hand-off. Five teams are on board and coverage scales as more join. 

  

Test runs depended on manual triggers, so results varied and could not be audited. Three Azure DevOps pipelines (validation, self-serve and regression) now run on parameters, and results publish even when tests fail. Setting up a new team took two to three days; a Node.js utility now builds the full project from one template in minutes, saving roughly 40 to 50 hours across the project. 

  

On Air Quality, unavailable third-party systems kept stopping testing. Always-on virtual services let us run more than 100 scenarios on demand, saving about 180 hours. Most IPAFFS and Risk Engine testers worked manually; we chose with them what to automate and merged scripts after domain-expert review. PDF validation for all four CHED types fell from ten minutes to two minutes. IDCOMS test data preparation, previously two person-days per cycle, was automated. 

  

Posit upgrades arrive every quarter across four environments. The framework stands at 40 per cent automated against a 90:10 target.
