## Cross-paper scope conflicts

- **257 person-days** is the headline in both AI (A4) and Innovation (I4). A common nomination boundary across the account, shared regression and specialist platform is not established. Judges may read this as one result entered twice. **Recommendation:** claim it in AI only.
- **85% accessibility** appears in both AI and Innovation. The NFT paper closes with "wider accessibility automation". Keep this figure in one paper only.
- **Governance wording diverges:** AI says "review by Delivery Excellence", Innovation says "approval by". Neither record is supplied. Align on "team-reported" until the export exists.
- **Inteliperf** (AI paper) and **PerfOps/Perf Insights** (NFT paper) are not distinguished. State whether they are separate tools.
- **Exhibits A01–A08** are reused in both AI and Innovation. They are illustrative only in each.
- **Third-party identity:** all three papers must supply the client identity to the organiser under organiser rules, recorded separately and never in the text. Anonymity in the text is not an exemption.
- **Limits:** the summaries appear to be under 100 words and the main texts under 14,600 characters. Confirm with a character count after revision.

## What editing can and cannot fix

**Editorial revision can fix:** overstated framing, scope bleed, the setup-time conflict (by omitting it) and double-counting. This may raise writing scores and avoid penalties.

**Only new evidence can raise substantiation scores:** benefits exports, timed baselines, release and incident windows, run histories, quality comparisons and deployment records. Repeated review of the same text adds no evidence.
