TESTA council review — GPT-5.6-SOL, round 1

Overall judgement

The drafts are unusually candid about limitations, but candour does not substitute for records. The AI and Innovation papers combine distinct populations under one nomination without proving a common project boundary. The NFT paper has the best criterion fit, yet its principal efficiency and release claims remain team-reported. None currently supports 4/5 on every criterion.

AI paper

Criterion scores: A1 measured quality improvement 2/5 — 257 person-days and one bounded task are reported, but no defect, accuracy, coverage-quality or before/after quality comparison exists. A2 originality 3/5 — governed prompts, deterministic rescoring and a documented QLoRA accessibility design show thoughtful adaptation, but several capabilities are prototypes and the served model is unproved. A3 sustained value/adaptability 2/5 — two quarters, team growth, versioned prompts and controls are described; matching usage/run records and comparative evaluation are absent. A4 efficiency/cost/waste 3/5 — 257 net person-days and eight-to-four hours are appropriately scoped, but no Pulse export, approval record or calculation is supplied. A5 stakeholder outcomes 1/5 — a useful assurance workflow is described, with no satisfaction measure or verified service-user outcome. Total 11/25. Writing 22/25: clear, disciplined and well bounded, but too much space goes to unvalidated product breadth.

Three biggest defects and replacements:

1. Passage: “Within the accessibility strand, we report 85 per cent automation of the activity covered by that result.” The undefined denominator, period and source make this unusable. Replace with: “The team has reported 85% activity coverage, but the underlying activity list, numerator, denominator and measurement period have not yet been supplied; we therefore do not rely on this figure as an outcome.”

2. Passage: “The account team reports 125 person-days saved in Q2 and 132 in Q3, after review time.” Replace with: “The account team recorded 125 person-days in Q2 and 132 in Q3 after deducting review time. These remain reported figures pending export of the underlying entries, calculation method and approval record.”

3. Passage: “The project has established an account delivery method … and a set of specialist capabilities for further development.” This joins account delivery and prototype products without establishing one project. Replace with: “This entry concerns the account AI delivery method. Specialist prototypes are included only as development context unless governance and run records establish that they formed part of the same delivery scope.”

Package now: the fine-tuning/design documentation; A01–A07 as clearly labelled local interface illustrations; A08 as 59 utility tests over synthetic fixtures; A09 as a dataset-integrity warning; the stated quarterly and monthly figures as unverified team reports. Still absent: Pulse export and approvals, calculation sheets, representative account runs, quality baseline/comparator, defined 85% population, deployed-model evidence, independent holdout and stakeholder/customer measures. A 4/5 on every criterion is not defensible.

Non-functional paper

Criterion scores: N1 requirements met/exceeded 3/5 — thresholds, profiles and failed P90 checks show a requirements mechanism, but successful closure and conflicting endpoint/aggregate results are not evidenced. N2 representative conditions 4/5 — production-informed workload design, AKS behaviour and cache-pattern reproduction are strong and specific, though source run artefacts should corroborate them. N3 release confidence 3/5 — testing is linked to migration and three releases, but dates, decision records and the post-release observation window are missing. N4 goals/resources/results 3/5 — goals, tools, roles and results are coherent; >90% effort reduction lacks before/after hours, and dashboard counts do not prove deployments. N5 challenges/response 4/5 — rebuilds, invalid exits, missing telemetry and cache behaviour receive concrete engineering responses, albeit with limited closure evidence. Total 17/25. Writing 23/25: the strongest paper, technically concrete and logically organised, with a few overextended claims.

Three biggest defects and replacements:

1. Passage: “The team reports that automation reduced manual performance-validation effort by over 90% and infrastructure-validation effort by over 90%.” Replace with: “The team reports reductions above 90% for two validation tasks; before/after hours, run frequency and the measurement period have not yet been supplied, so these percentages remain unverified.”

2. Passage: “Performance testing supplied readiness evidence for three major releases … The team reports no production issues following those releases.” Replace with: “The team reports that testing informed three release decisions. Release dates, sign-off records, issue criteria and the observation window are required before claiming an issue-free production outcome.”

3. Passage: “One performance report identifies 22 endpoints exceeding the P95 SLA.” Replace with: “One report flags 22 endpoint-level P95 breaches. Before submission, reconcile these with aggregate results and package the transaction-level thresholds, run identifier and investigation disposition.”

Package now: workload capture showing 7,532/7,200 requests and about 2.1/2 RPS; failed P90 alert; redacted cache reproduction/remediation material; missing-telemetry result (57/64 missing, 11% available); deterministic-rule description and 285 tooling tests; dashboard only as status-view evidence. Still absent: task-level time studies, successful reruns/defect closure, reconciled P95 data, release sign-offs/dates/observation window, migration decision record and redacted primary run artefacts. Do not upload confidential originals. A universal 4/5 is not defensible; N2 and N5 can reach 4 with the present narrative, but the others need records.

Innovation paper

Criterion scores: I1 advancement of practice 3/5 — distributed ownership plus shared regression is useful organisational innovation, but uses established technology and lacks comparative results. I2 driver/implementation 3/5 — bottleneck, scaffolding, three pipelines and ownership are well explained, without implementation run records. I3 industry boundaries 2/5 — the combination is credible but external adoption, publication, novel technical evidence and deployed AI extensions are absent. I4 goals/resources/results 2/5 — five-team adoption and setup speed are asserted; no repository/pipeline/onboarding evidence or outcomes are supplied, while 257 days belongs to a separate scope. I5 obstacles/response 3/5 — maintenance, triage, setup and governance constraints have plausible responses but limited evidence of effectiveness. Total 13/25. Writing 22/25: honest and readable, but diluted by the separate AI programme.

Three biggest defects and replacements:

1. Passage: “Five service teams are reported as onboarded.” Replace with: “The team reports five onboarded services; onboarding records, tagged-test inventories and shared-suite run history are still required to substantiate adoption.”

2. Passage: “The team reports that generation now takes minutes.” Replace with: “The generator reportedly reduces project creation from two to three days to minutes. Define whether the baseline covers project scaffolding alone and provide timestamped examples; do not present this as complete onboarding time.”

3. Passage: “Across the separate account AI delivery scope … totalling 257 after review time.” Remove this paragraph. Replace with: “AI work is a separate delivery scope and is excluded from the innovation result unless a common nomination boundary and direct contribution to the shared regression model can be evidenced.”

Package now: architecture/strategy descriptions, template and pipeline design, responsibility model, and any existing redacted utility output as implementation illustrations; AI exhibits only as explicitly separate prototype context. Still absent: five-team onboarding records, commit/tag inventories, pipeline run histories, setup timing samples, suite growth and maintenance data, defects/cross-service regressions found, contributor feedback and external adoption. A 4/5 on every criterion is not defensible.

Cross-paper conflicts

The 257 person-days and eight-to-four-hour coverage task recur in AI and Innovation, risking double counting and category dilution. The AI account, shared-regression platform and specialist applications are distinct populations until records prove a common boundary. “Two to three days to minutes” must stay limited to scaffolding and must not be combined with an earlier 40–50-hour or 99% claim. In NFT, 11 dashboard projects are not 11 successful deployments; 285 tests validate analysis tooling, not client scenarios; 1,200 Sonar projects is estate size, not evaluated coverage. Keep A08/A09 limitations visible across papers: interface and utility evidence does not prove deployed model quality or customer impact.
