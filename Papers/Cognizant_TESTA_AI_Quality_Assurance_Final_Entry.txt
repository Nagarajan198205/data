Summary

Cognizant Worldwide Limited UK introduced AI assistance into test delivery for a UK government client using approved tools, reusable prompts and review before use. The account narrative reports 257 person-days of released effort in Q2 and Q3 2026, after deducting review time. A monthly assurance task reportedly fell from eight hours to four within that total. Separately scoped specialist applications explore accessibility, security and performance interpretation. Engineers retain responsibility for checking generated material and technical findings. The entry distinguishes reported delivery benefits from documented designs and local demonstrations.

1 Quality goals and the delivery approach

Our test services team at Cognizant Worldwide Limited UK needed to reduce repetitive preparation for a UK government client without weakening review. Test design, documentation and automation scripting were the initial focus. The client selected and approved the available tools, so our approach had to work within that environment. We began with a planning quarter and introduced AI into test plans during Q2 2026.

Microsoft 365 Copilot supported the first delivery phase. The account narrative describes training for associates then on the account and the creation of reusable prompts. GitHub Copilot extended the approach to automation coding in Q3. Engineers checked AI-assisted deliverables before they entered the test management system, including whether test cases addressed expected behaviour and negative scenarios. Review remained part of the work.

The account team reports 125 person-days of released effort in Q2 and 132 in Q3, after review time. These figures describe AI-assisted delivery across the account (S01). Cognizant Intelligent Platform is a separately scoped development strand covering IntelliA11y, IntelliSec and Inteliperf. No common client deployment is established by grouping those applications under a platform name.

The accessibility team reports 85 per cent automation within its workflow. The reported coverage combines repeatable rules and AI-assisted interpretation, while specialists examine potential barriers and verify corrections. It is an activity-coverage claim, with its calculation and measurement period still to be substantiated, rather than a measure of model accuracy or accessibility conformance.

2 Applying AI to specific quality decisions

The Code Coverage Agent uses GitHub Copilot to assemble analysis and reporting for a Sonar estate of more than 1,200 projects; the number of projects evaluated is not stated in the source. The account narrative says the client assurance team uses it to review coverage, quality indicators and maintenance needs. Bringing those observations together gives the reviewer a starting point for examining gaps across the estate.

A prototype Test Impact Analyser explores a more selective regression approach. Its documented design considers a change alongside requirements, dependencies, risks and existing tests, and provides for publishing recommendations and proposed test cases into Azure DevOps. The documented design uses fixed code to recalculate scores after model interpretation. It also separates team data and caches repeated requests. The design supports batches of up to 200 change identifiers. Delivery savings from this prototype are not included in our reported total.

IntelliA11y addresses the interpretation of accessibility findings. A rule can detect a missing label, while assessing whether a label is meaningful requires the surrounding interaction. The documented development approach specifies adaptation of Phi-4 for accessibility using quantised low-rank adaptation, or QLoRA, under the identifier phi4:14b-a11y. It covers finding classification, proposed remediation and severity assessment. Retrieved guidance supplies reference context separately from model adaptation. Source inspection confirms a QLoRA training implementation with adapter saving and evaluation-loss reporting (A10). A completed training run and a served adapted model remain to be established.

The accessibility design covers forms, keyboard interaction, screen-reader experience, visual presentation and cognitive load. Exhibits A03 and A04 show a local platform introduction and knowledge interface; they do not show model identity or an accessibility analysis run. The reviewed workspace did not establish that the documented adapted model served a client run. A09 records overlap in a local training/evaluation split, so that split cannot substantiate independent model uplift.

IntelliSec assists investigation and prioritisation across code, application, dependency and cloud findings. Engineers confirm the suspected weakness and its relevance. Local exhibits A01 and A02 show the investigation and assessment interfaces, including functions labelled as planned. The A01 figures are local evaluation data, not client results. Inteliperf helps interpret performance measurements against workload intent, with AI assistance separated from deterministic execution. Its documented boundaries prohibit invented endpoints, silent scenario changes and release decisions made by AI alone. Exhibits A05 to A07 illustrate local interfaces and score descriptions in a demonstration workspace; they do not show client runs.
